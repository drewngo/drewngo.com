progress is a double-edged sword
2026-11-26

ive always enjoyed technology, specifically in the world of computing (hence my choice of computer engineering as a major). one of the particularly fascinating things to me is the innovation and improvement we've made throughout the years. 

the most prominent advancement in technology without a doubt has to be artificial intelligence. and it's not even technically a new topic. Alan Turing introduced core ideas in the mid 1900s, and today, it is genuinely advancing at a ridiculous pace.

i recently rewatched the movie hidden figures (2016) to relive some history as i prepare to start my internship at NASA Langley in the spring. there is a scene that ive been thinking about for the past few days, where Katherine Johnson is reassigned from the space task group because of the IBM computer that performed calculations much faster than a human computer, thus making her abilities seem 'unnecessary'. when she's told this news by the director, he says "progress is a double-edged sword". 

this quote made me ponder the circumstances that i live in. ive enjoyed technology, and the engineering of technology, because WE built it. WE went and made rocks think. WE went and sent people to the moon. today, we see entire projects and products, built from one prompt. we are arguably only in the stone age of AI, and it's creating incredible things, advancing engineering and science as a whole at a rate i dont think we've ever seen before. however, for some reason, i cant love it the same way i love Ada Lovelace, the first computer programmer. i cant love it the same way i love the Wright brothers, the inventors of the airplane.

i wont sit here and act like i dont use AI. another scene in the movie that stuck with me was when Dorothy Vaughan saw how replaceable she and her girls were in computing, because of the same IBM computer. she took it upon herself to learn how to program it, and taught and brought her girls along with her. like Dorothy, i know i have to adapt. i feel that if i dont learn to use and understand AI, i'll fall behind. so i'll learn it. but every step of progress made, the double-edged sword seems to cut away a little more of why i fell in love with building things in the first place.
